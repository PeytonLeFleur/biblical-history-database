## Findings
**Settled by the evidence:**
- **The direction of Necho's march.** WEB and KJV say "against the king of Assyria"; NKJV says "to the aid of".
  - ABC 3 records Egypt fighting for Aššur-uballiṭ in 610–609, and Josephus independently has Necho marching against the Medes and Babylonians: independently corroborated.
  - Lumby (1887), writing before ABC 3 was published, read the expedition as against Assyria. That reading is recorded as superseded.

**Disputed (queued for review):**
- **Place of death:** at Megiddo (Kings) or after reaching Jerusalem (2 Chr 35:24; Josephus).
- **The law book:** an ancient Mosaic text (Josephus, Keil) or a seventh-century Deuteronomy (Driver, after de Wette). Both sides agree it contained Deuteronomy, which is recorded as historically plausible.
- **Timing of the reform:** all within the 18th year (Ussher, as reported by Keil) or spread over several years (Keil; 2 Chr 34:3; Josephus).

**Recorded without harmonising:**
- Josephus names the high priest **Eliakim**, not Hilkiah.
- Josephus dates the Bethel prophecy's fulfillment 361 years after it was spoken; Guzik (citing Knapp) says "more than three hundred years".
- Guzik identifies Jeremiah's father with the high priest Hilkiah. Not adopted; left as an open question.

**Herodotus 2.159:** "Magdolos" is a confusion with Megiddo (Keil); Movers placed it at Migdol on the Egyptian frontier (as reported by Keil). Classified historically plausible.

**Reclassified honestly:** the claim that Josiah died in 609 was lowered to historically plausible, because only ABC 3 supports the year directly.
