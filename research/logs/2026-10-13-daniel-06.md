## Findings worth noting
- **Irrevocable law:**
  - The "law of the Medes and Persians, which doesn't alter" is attested only in Daniel and Esther (1:19; 8:8).
  - Diodorus 17.30, commonly cited for it, says only that Darius III's power could not undo an execution already carried out. It mentions **no law**.
  - Classified: no independent corroboration found.
- **Guzik's Ammianus quotation:** Rolfe's translation reads "detestable" where Guzik's quotation has "formidable". The content (all relatives put to death) matches.
- **Captive lions:** first proposed as independently corroborated. The validator rejected that because the evidence is only a Wikipedia summary of the Assyrian reliefs. It is now **historically plausible**, with that limitation stated (fix06).

## Claims
- **Independently corroborated:**
  - Persian rule through appointed provincial governors (ABC 7 iii 20, *Cyropaedia*, Herodotus, Behistun)
  - "satrap" as an Old Persian title
- **Disputed, review queued:** the 120 satraps. Wilson reads them as sub-governors in Babylonia; critics see Darius I's satrapy system projected back.
- **Historically plausible:**
  - captive lions kept by Mesopotamian kings (Assyrian, secondary)
  - collective punishment of families (Herodotus, Ammianus)
- **No independent corroboration found:**
  - the irrevocable law
  - the thirty-day decree
  - Daniel as one of three chief officials
  - Darius's decree
- **Not empirically testable:** the angel shutting the lions' mouths.
