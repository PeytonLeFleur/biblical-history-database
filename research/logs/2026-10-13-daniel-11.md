# Session log: 2026-10-13, Daniel 11 (Milestone 5, session 11)

**Unit:** Daniel chapter 11 (45 verses). All verses reviewed; 6 candidates.
- 11:1 ends the ch. 10 speech and is linked to the Dan 10 event.
- 11:2–45 are recorded as five prophecy sections attached to the "vision by the Tigris" record.

## Separation of layers
- **Text:** Daniel names Persia, Greece, Egypt, "the glorious land", Kittim, Edom, Moab and Ammon, but **no king**. Each prophecy record's `in_text_interpretation` says so.
- **The Dan 10 experience event** now also mentions 11:2–45 as the narrated revelation. Its revision is re-audited.
- **Five prophecy records, 14 interpretations:**
  1. **Four kings of Persia (11:2):** Cambyses, Smerdis, Darius I and Xerxes (Jerome; Driver), or Cyrus to Xerxes (Newton; Guzik).
  2. **A mighty king and the fourfold division (11:3–4):** Alexander and the successors.
  3. **Kings of the south and north (11:5–20):**
     - Ptolemies and Seleucids from Ptolemy I to Seleucus IV. Porphyry and Jerome agreed on this section, and Newton, Driver and Guzik follow it.
     - Variants at 11:20: Porphyry (Ptolemy V); "the Hebrews" (Trypho); **Guzik names "Seleucus III"**. The Babylonian king list shows the son who succeeded Antiochus III was Seleucus IV.
     - Jerome on 11:14: the Onias temple.
  4. **The contemptible person (11:21–35):**
     - Antiochus IV (Porphyry, Driver, Montgomery, Guzik)
     - the Antichrist with Antiochus as a type (Jerome's school; not a fulfillment claim)
     - Jewish readings via Jerome: the Romans, Titus, Julian
     - Newton: Rome from 11:31; Constantine as the "little help"
  5. **The wilful king (11:36–45):**
     - Antiochus continued. Driver: 11:40–45 "do not correspond" to the known end; Porphyry's fourth Egyptian campaign is known only through Jerome.
     - a future Antichrist (Jerome; Jews of his day; Guzik, with the transition at 11:36)
     - Newton: the Byzantine empire, with the Saracens and Turks

  - The desecration is after Eleusis (1 Macc; Jerome).
  - The rededication is 36–38 months after the desecration (1 Macc 4:54; Driver's "3 years and 10 days").
  - All solve consistently: desecration 168–166 BC, rededication 165–163 BC.
- **Not modelled:** whether Antiochus died before or after the rededication. 1 Macc 6 and 2 Macc 9–10 disagree, so this is recorded as competing reconstructions.
