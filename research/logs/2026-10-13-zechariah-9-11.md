## New records
### Event: the first "burden" of Zechariah 9–11
An undated oracle collection.

**Disputed (review): date and authorship**

| View | Proponents | Dating |
|---|---|---|
| Zechariah himself, after 518 | Keil | — |
| Greek period | Mitchell | 9:1–10 in 333 BC, just after Issus (Tyre not yet taken; 9:8 an interpolation reflecting Alexander's sparing of Jerusalem); 9:11–11:3 under Ptolemy III (247–222); 11:4–17 soon after Raphia (217), by another author |
| Pre-exilic | older critics, answered by Keil | — |

**Strongly supported (historical):** Alexander's army took Damascus (333), Tyre (Hecatombaion 332) and Gaza (332).
- Arrian, *Anabasis* book 2, read directly.
- Josephus, *Ant.* 11.8.3–4, read directly: seven months for Tyre, two for Gaza.
- Whether Zech 9 refers to these events is recorded only as an interpretation.

### Prophecy records (4), each with attributed interpretations
- **The burden on Hadrach–Philistia (9:1–8)**
  - Alexander's march: Hengstenberg via Keil (Keil: only the beginning; Koehler's objection on 9:7) and Guzik.
  - Written in 333: Mitchell, following Kuiper.
- **The lowly king on a donkey (9:9–10)**
  - Fulfilled in Jesus's entry: Matt 21; John 12; Keil; Guzik.
  - An ideal king prompted by Alexander: Mitchell (idealist).
- **Sons of Zion against sons of Greece; the regathering (9:11–11:3)**
  - Maccabean beginnings: Keil, Guzik.
  - Ptolemaic date, with "O Greece" a gloss: Mitchell.
- **The shepherd allegory and the thirty pieces of silver (11:4–17)**
  - Judas and the potter's field (Matt 27:3–10): Keil, Guzik. Keil reads Matthew's "Jeremiah" via Jer 18–19.
  - A Ptolemy III parable: Mitchell. Mitchell calls the Jeremiah attribution a misattribution.

## Correction (same PR)
The first draft attributed to Mitchell a date for 9:11–11:3 "under the early Ptolemies". That misread his discussion of Philadelphus. His own summary (Introduction, pp. 258–259) gives:
- 9:11–11:3: the reign of Ptolemy III (247–222).
- 11:4–17 with 13:7–9: a third writer, soon after Raphia (217).
- 12:1–13:6 and ch. 14: a fourth, apocalyptic writer, about the same time.

The prophecy interpretation, the event context, the claim reasoning and this log were corrected before merge. No record revision was bumped, since no decision had been made on any review.
