# The refusal of Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego and the fiery furnace (Dan 3:8–27)

> Programmatic validation is not historical verification. Classifications are machine-proposed unless marked as owner-reviewed.

## Biblical references

- Daniel 3:8–27 (narration)

## Biblical account

Chaldeans denounced Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego for not serving the king's gods. Given a second chance, they declared that their God could deliver them, and that even if he did not they would not worship the image. The furnace was heated seven times hotter, the bound men were thrown in, the men who threw them died, and the king saw four men walking unharmed in the fire, the fourth "like a son of the gods". The three came out untouched by the fire.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Reign of Nebuchadnezzar II

Burning appears as a penalty in Old Babylonian law, and Jeremiah 29:22 says a king of Babylon "roasted in the fire" two Judean prophets in exile. [1]

### Rulers

- Nebuchadnezzar II: King who ordered the execution

## Competing reconstructions

- **The fourth figure as an angel** (The text (3:28: "sent his angel")): The king himself later says God sent his angel.
- **The fourth figure as a Christophany** (D. Guzik and many Christian interpreters): Reading "like the Son of God", the figure is the pre-incarnate Christ present with the faithful in their trial. [2]
- **Translation note** (English versions): Some English versions render 3:25 "a son of the gods" (the king's polytheistic idiom, as in the WEB), others "the Son of God"; the difference affects interpretation, not the narrated event.

## Claims and evidence

### Burning was used as a punishment in Mesopotamian law and is attributed to a king of Babylon in Jeremiah 29:22.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

The Code of Hammurabi prescribes burning for several offences; Jeremiah 29:22 (a biblical, not independent, source) says a king of Babylon roasted two prophets in the fire. No Neo-Babylonian legal text was consulted.

- *Supporting (indirect):* The Code of Hammurabi prescribes burning for looting at a house fire (§25), for a consecrated woman entering a tavern (§110), and for incest with one's mother (§157). (§§25, 110, 157) [1]

*Limitations:* Hammurabi's code is about 1,100 years earlier.

### Shadrach, Meshach and Abednego refused to worship the image and were thrown into a furnace.

**Classification:** No independent corroboration found (machine proposed) · layer: text

No independent record of these men or this execution was found. This is not a disproof.

- *Context (indirect):* The Code of Hammurabi prescribes burning for looting at a house fire (§25), for a consecrated woman entering a tavern (§110), and for incest with one's mother (§157). (§§25, 110, 157) [1]

### The three survived the furnace unharmed, accompanied by a fourth figure.

**Classification:** Not empirically testable (machine proposed) · layer: text

A miraculous deliverance cannot be assessed by historical or scientific methods; evidence about Babylonian punishments bears only on the setting. This is not a theological verdict.
