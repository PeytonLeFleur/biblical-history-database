## Biblical account

In Belshazzar's third year Daniel sees himself at the citadel of Susa by the Ulai. A two-horned ram is overthrown by a swift goat whose great horn is broken and replaced by four; from one comes a little horn that attacks the host of heaven, removes the continual offering and casts down the sanctuary for 2,300 evenings and mornings. Gabriel explains that the ram is the kings of Media and Persia and the goat the king of Greece, and foretells a fierce king who will be broken without human hand. Daniel falls ill, then returns to the king's business.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Narrative setting: Belshazzar's regency (third year); composition date disputed (see Daniel 7)

Susa was the capital of Elam and later a principal residence of the Persian kings (Neh 1:1; Esth 1:2). In Belshazzar's time it lay outside Babylonian control. The Ulai was a stream or canal at Susa; its course is not precisely identified (Pleiades marks the candidate river with a question mark). [1, 2]

## Claims and evidence

### Alexander of Macedon overthrew the Persian empire (Arbela, 331 BC; death of Darius III, 330 BC).

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Arrian dates the decisive battle to the archonship of Aristophanes (331/330) and Darius's death to that of Aristophon (330/329). This is the historical background against which 8:5–7 and 8:21 are read; it is not itself an identification of the symbols.

- *Supporting (direct):* Arbela fought in the archonship of Aristophanes, month Pyanepsion; Darius III died of wounds before Alexander saw him, archonship of Aristophon. (3.15; 3.21–22) [6]

### After Alexander, four of his successors (Cassander, Lysimachus, Ptolemy, Seleucus) acted as kings in alliance against Antigonus.

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Diodorus describes the four-king coalition before Ipsus. Jerome lists a different four, so the "four kingdoms" of 8:22 are matched to different sets by different interpreters.

- *Supporting (direct):* Cassander summoned Lysimachus; Ptolemy and Seleucus agreed to join them against Antigonus. (20.106.2–5) [7]
- *Context (indirect):* Jerome's four successors are Ptolemy, Philip Arrhidaeus, Seleucus and Antigonus. (on 8:8, 22) [8]

### Daniel was physically at Susa when he saw the vision.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

Guzik reads 8:27 ("the king's business") as Daniel being in Shushan on official business; Driver reads 8:2 as describing where Daniel saw himself in the vision. In Belshazzar's time Susa lay outside Babylonian rule, which bears on the question but was not researched further.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Daniel "was in Shushan on business for the king". (on 8:2, 27) [5]
- *Challenging (indirect):* The verse describes where Daniel "seemed to find himself in the vision". (p. 111 (on 8:2)) [1]

### Alexander was shown the Book of Daniel at Jerusalem.

**Classification:** No independent corroboration found (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Reported only by Josephus. Arrian's narrative of the campaign from Tyre to Gaza and Egypt does not mention Jerusalem.

- *Supporting (direct):* Alexander was shown the Book of Daniel and supposed he was the Greek who would destroy the Persian empire. (Ant. 11.8.5) [9]
- *Context (direct):* Arbela fought in the archonship of Aristophanes, month Pyanepsion; Darius III died of wounds before Alexander saw him, archonship of Aristophon. (3.15; 3.21–22) [6]

*Limitations:* Absence in Arrian is not disproof.

### Gabriel explained the vision to Daniel.

**Classification:** Not empirically testable (machine proposed) · layer: text

An angelic revelation cannot be assessed historically. This is not a theological verdict.
