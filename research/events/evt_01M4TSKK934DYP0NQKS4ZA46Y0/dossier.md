## Biblical account

Daniel dreams in Belshazzar's first year and writes it down. Four beasts rise from the sea; the fourth has ten horns and a boastful little horn. Thrones are set and the Ancient of Days judges; the beast is destroyed; one like a son of man comes with the clouds and receives an everlasting kingdom. An attendant explains: the beasts are four kings; the fourth is a kingdom whose ten horns are kings, after whom another will persecute the saints for a time, times and half a time; then judgment gives the kingdom to the saints of the Most High.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Narrative setting: the regency of Belshazzar (from c. Nabonidus year 3, 553 BC); composition date disputed

Belshazzar governed Babylonia while Nabonidus was in Arabia: the Verse Account says Nabonidus "entrusted the kingship" to his firstborn "when the third year was about to begin", and the Chronicle has the king in Tayma and "the prince" in Akkad in years 7, 9, 10 and 11. No Babylonian document counts years of Belshazzar, so Daniel's "first year" cannot be converted to a calendar date without assuming a counting basis; this is modelled separately. Critical scholars date the composition of the chapter to the Antiochene crisis (c. 167–164 BC), traditional scholars to the 6th century BC. [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6]

### Rulers

- Belshazzar: Regent ("king" in Daniel) [1, 3]
- Nabonidus: King of Babylon (in Tayma) [2]

## Competing reconstructions

- **Undated (model-independent)** (This project's default): Without a Babylonian count of Belshazzar's years the vision is placed only before Belshazzar's death (Oct 539). [4]
- **Counted from the entrusting of the kingship (c. 553 BC)** (R. P. Dougherty (1929) on the regency's start; counting basis assumed here): If Daniel counts from Nabonidus's delegation of kingship, the first year falls c. 553–551 BC. Shown only in the "Belshazzar's years" model. [1, 3, 2]
- **Composition in the Maccabean crisis** (S. R. Driver (1900), J. J. Collins (1984), via Wikipedia): The vision was written c. 167–164 BC, before the Temple's rededication; the date formula is literary. [5, 6]
- **Composition in the 6th century BC** (Jerome, R. D. Wilson (1917), D. Guzik): The vision was seen and written by Daniel in Belshazzar's reign, as stated. [7, 8, 9]

## Claims and evidence

### Nabonidus entrusted the kingship in Babylonia to Belshazzar around the start of his third year (c. 553 BC).

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

The Verse Account says so directly ("when the third year was about to begin"); the Chronicle places the western campaign in year 3 and the king in Tayma, with the prince in Akkad, in years 7–11; Smith's correlation is reported by Dougherty.

- *Supporting (direct):* "When the third year was about to begin" Nabonidus entrusted the army to his eldest son and "entrusted the kingship to him". (col. ii 5–6 (Livius numbering)) [1]
- *Supporting (direct):* Year 3: campaign to Amurru and Edom (Abu–Kislimu); years 7–11: the king in Tayma, the prince in Akkad. (i 11–17 (year 3); ii 5 ff. (years 7, 9–11)) [2]
- *Context (indirect):* Smith showed the Verse Account's Tema campaign accords with the Chronicle's 3rd year; Dougherty treats Belshazzar as co-regent from then. (pp. 105–107) [3]

*Limitations:* The Verse Account is hostile polemic; the Chronicle's years 4–5 are lost. Beaulieu (1989) not read.

### Daniel's "first year of Belshazzar" can be fixed to a calendar year.

**Classification:** No independent corroboration found (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

No Babylonian text dates by Belshazzar's years. A date (c. 553–551 BC) follows only if Daniel counted from the entrusting of the kingship; that assumption is modelled separately and the event is otherwise left undated.

- *Context (direct):* "When the third year was about to begin" Nabonidus entrusted the army to his eldest son and "entrusted the kingship to him". (col. ii 5–6 (Livius numbering)) [1]
- *Context (direct):* Nabonidus year 2 began 19 Apr 554, year 3 on 7 Apr 553, year 4 on 26 Apr 552; no Belshazzar years appear in the dated documents. (Year tables (Nabonidus)) [4]

### Daniel saw this vision and wrote it down in Belshazzar's first year.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: text

Traditional interpreters accept the date formula; critical scholars date the chapter to the Antiochene crisis (c. 167–164 BC) because its horizon ends with the little horn. The disagreement concerns authorship and date of composition and is unresolved.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Jerome treats the vision as seen in Belshazzar's first year and defends the book's prophetic authorship against Porphyry. (on 7:1; prologue) [7]
- *Challenging (indirect):* The age of Antiochus Epiphanes is "the limiting horizon of the book"; the visions culminate in his persecution. (pp. 98–100) [5]
- *Context (indirect):* Reports the critical dating of the visions to 167–164 BC, with ch. 7 somewhat earlier. (Composition (Collins 1984)) [6]

*Limitations:* Collins known here only via Wikipedia.

### Antiochus IV reigned over the Seleucid empire from Ulûlu 137 SE (175 BC) to Kislîmu 148 SE (late 164 BC).

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

The Babylonian King List gives his accession and death report; 1 Maccabees gives the same accession year and his death in 149 (Macedonian-reckoned era).

- *Supporting (direct):* Antiochus ascended in Ulûlu of year 137; in Kislîmu of year 148 "it was heard that" he had died. (rev. 9–14) [10]
- *Supporting (direct):* Reign from year 137; altar desecrated 15 Chislev 145; rededicated 25 Chislev 148; died in 149. (1 Macc 1:10, 54; 4:52–54; 6:16) [11]

*Limitations:* King List translation flagged for revision by its editor.

### Antiochus IV suppressed Temple worship in Jerusalem for about three years (Chislev 145 – Chislev 148 SE).

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

1 Maccabees, independent of Daniel, dates the "abomination of desolation" to 15 Chislev 145 and the rededication to 25 Chislev 148, with persecuting decrees before the desecration. It is a single (Jewish) source tradition in this session: non-Jewish confirmation (Diodorus 34/35) could not be accessed, and Polybius attests only Antiochus's notoriety.

- *Supporting (direct):* Reign from year 137; altar desecrated 15 Chislev 145; rededicated 25 Chislev 148; died in 149. (1 Macc 1:10, 54; 4:52–54; 6:16) [11]
- *Context (indirect):* His conduct earned him the nickname Epimanes, "madman". (26.1–1a) [12]

*Limitations:* One source tradition read (1 Maccabees); 2 Maccabees and Diodorus not read.

### Interpreters before the Christian era read the "one like a son of man" as an individual figure.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

The Parables of Enoch present "that Son of Man" as an individual beside the Head of Days; Charles dates them c. 105–64 BC, but other scholars date them later, possibly after the start of the Christian era.

- *Supporting (direct):* Enoch sees one with "a head of days" and "that Son of Man" who has righteousness and will raise kings from their seats. (1 Enoch 46:1–4) [13]

*Limitations:* Dating of the Parables disputed (Driver reports Dillmann, Charles, Schürer).
