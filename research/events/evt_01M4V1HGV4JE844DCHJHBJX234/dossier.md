## Biblical account

In Cyrus's third year Daniel mourns three weeks; on the 24th of the first month, by the Tigris, he sees a dazzling man in linen whom his companions do not see. He falls senseless, is raised by a hand, and told that his prayer was heard from the first day but the prince of Persia withstood the messenger 21 days until Michael helped. Strengthened again, he is told that the messenger will return to fight the prince of Persia, that the prince of Greece will come, and that he will reveal what is written in the writing of truth.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Early Persian rule in Babylonia: Cyrus's third Babylonian year (spring 536 – spring 535)

Two years after Cyrus's first-year decree (Ezra 1), Daniel is still in Babylonia. The 24th of the first month (Nisan) falls just after Passover and the feast of Unleavened Bread (14–21 Nisan), which on Driver's reading lay within his three weeks of mourning. Hiddekel is the Tigris (Gen 2:14). [1, 2]

## Claims and evidence

### "King of Persia" was not a title borne by the Persian kings while their empire lasted, so its use in Dan 10:1 reflects a later writer.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

Driver argues the title was given to Persian kings only after the empire, to distinguish them from Greek rulers. The Behistun inscription (§1, Livius translation) has Darius call himself "the king of Persia" among his titles (other renderings: "king in Persia"). The Hebrew usage in Ezra and Daniel versus royal Persian titulary is not settled by these two sources.

- *Supporting (indirect):* "King of Persia": a title not borne by the Persian kings while the empire lasted, though often given them afterwards. (p. 152 (on 10:1)) [2]
- *Challenging (direct):* Darius: "the great king, king of kings, the king of Persia, the king of countries" (Livius translation). (DB §1) [7]

*Limitations:* Old Persian wording not checked directly.

### Daniel saw a heavenly messenger and heard of conflicts among heavenly princes.

**Classification:** Not empirically testable (machine proposed) · layer: text

Visionary and angelic experience cannot be assessed historically. This is not a theological verdict.


### The vision's date (24th of the first month, Cyrus's third year) fell just after Passover and Unleavened Bread, around April 536 BC.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Driver identifies the first month as Nisan, so the festivals of 14–21 Nisan fell within the three weeks; Parker & Dubberstein's table gives 1 Nisan of Cyrus's year 3 as 30 Mar 536, so the 24th ≈ 22 Apr 536 (operator arithmetic, Babylonian calendar).

- *Supporting (direct):* 1 Nisan of Cyrus's year 3 = 30 Mar 536 BC. (Cyrus table, year 3) [1]

*Limitations:* Assumes Babylonian Nisan reckoning for both the year and the month.

## Commentary (interpretation, not historical evidence)

- Guzik notes that Daniel, about 84, stayed in Babylon after the first return, reads the three weeks as a partial fast, takes the prince of Persia as a high-ranking demon resisting the answer to prayer, and leans toward identifying the man in linen as an angel rather than Christ. [5]

## Unanswered questions and research gaps

- Old Persian wording of DB §1 not checked.
- Deut 32:8 at Qumran not read directly.
- Old Greek of Daniel 10 not read.
- Old Greek and Theodotion differences in ch. 10 not read beyond Jerome's notes.
- Deut 32:8 in 4QDeut(j) ("sons of God") not read directly.

## Disputes

- "King of Persia" was not a title borne by the Persian kings while their empire lasted, so its use in Dan 10:1 reflects a later writer.
