# Composition of the book of Daniel (date and authorship debated)

## Biblical references

- Daniel 7:1 (reference): "Then he wrote the dream and told the sum of the matters."
- Daniel 12:4 (reference): "Shut up the words and seal the book."
- Matthew 24:15 (reference): "Spoken of through Daniel the prophet."

## Biblical account

The book names no author in a heading. Chapters 1–6 tell of Daniel and his companions in the third person; chapters 7–12 report Daniel's visions largely in the first person; 7:1 says he wrote the dream down, and 8:26 and 12:4, 9 speak of sealing the vision or book until the time of the end. 2:4b–7:28 is in Aramaic, the rest in Hebrew.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Narrative setting 605–536 BC; proposed composition dates range from the late sixth century to c. 164 BC

The question was already argued in antiquity: Porphyry (3rd century AD) held the book was written under Antiochus Epiphanes and narrated past events, and Eusebius, Apollinaris and Methodius replied (Jerome). Modern critical scholarship (Driver; Montgomery) dates the final composition, at least of chs. 7–12, to 168–164 BC; conservative scholarship (Wilson; Pusey, Hengstenberg and others not read here) defends composition by Daniel in the sixth century. A Qumran copy, 4QDanᶜ (4Q114), has a radiocarbon range of 230–160 BCE (Popović et al. 2025). [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]

## Competing reconstructions

- **Sixth century BC, by Daniel (traditional)** (R. D. Wilson (1917): a date "say 535 B.C." remains tenable, Jerome (against Porphyry), D. Guzik (quoting Baldwin), K. A. Kitchen (1965), T. C. Mitchell & R. Joyce (1965): the language does not exclude an early date): Scholarly arguments: the linguistic evidence cannot decide the date (Kitchen; Mitchell & Joyce); alleged historical errors rest on silence and on mistaken assumptions about regnal reckoning (Wilson); Babylonian details such as Belshazzar's kingship fit a writer close to events (Wilson); Josephus reports the book shown to Alexander (Ant. 11.8.5); the radiocarbon range of 4Q114 begins before the Maccabean crisis. Theological commitments (stated separately): Jesus calls Daniel a prophet (Matt 24:15; Mark 13:14); predictive prophecy is possible; Baldwin (via Guzik) argues that a late date would make the visions fiction. [3, 4, 6, 7, 8]
- **Final composition c. 167–164 BC (critical)** (Porphyry (per Jerome), S. R. Driver (1900), J. A. Montgomery (1927, for chs. 7–12)): Scholarly arguments: Greek instrument names (3:5) and Persian loanwords; the book stands among the Writings, not the Prophets; Ben Sira (c. 200 BC) omits Daniel; detailed accuracy in ch. 11 to the persecution of Antiochus, then divergence at 11:40–45; developed angelology and resurrection; historical difficulties (Dan 1:1, Belshazzar as king, Darius the Mede). Theological stance (stated separately): Driver held that a late date does not deny the book's religious value. [1, 2, 4]
- **Two stages: tales of chs. 1–6 in the 3rd century (Babylonia); visions of chs. 7–12 in 168–165 BC** (J. A. Montgomery (1927)): The stories are pre-Maccabean, compiled from Jewish and Babylonian traditions after the Diadochi; the four visions were written seriatim in the first years of the Maccabean revolt. Montgomery notes he broke with the view that the whole book is Maccabean. [2]

## Claims and evidence

### A manuscript containing Daniel 8–11 (4QDanᶜ, 4Q114) was copied between about 230 and 160 BC.

Radiocarbon dating of the cleaned specimen (2σ, 230–160 BCE) in a peer-reviewed study. The calibrated range is not the complete range reported, and radiocarbon dates the parchment, not the composition.

- *Supporting (direct):* Accepted 2σ calibrated range 230–160 BCE; overlaps the period in which the final part of Daniel was "presumably authored". (4Q114 entry) [5]

*Limitations:* A single specimen; calibration ranges are probabilistic.

### The book of Daniel was composed in the sixth century BC by Daniel.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

Scholarly arguments on both sides are recorded separately from theological commitments. For an early date: the historical objections are arguments from silence or from reckoning conventions (Wilson); Josephus's Alexander tradition presupposes the book in the fourth century; 4Q114's radiocarbon range begins before the persecution. For a late date: language (Greek instrument names, Persian words), the book's place among the Writings, Ben Sira's silence, and the precision of ch. 11 up to the persecution with divergence at 11:40–45 (Driver; Montgomery; Porphyry). Montgomery dates the stories earlier than the visions. The radiocarbon range overlaps both views and does not decide them. Theological positions (Jesus' reference to "Daniel the prophet"; the possibility of predictive prophecy) are noted on the dossier and not weighed as historical evidence. The linguistic argument is itself contested: Kitchen (1965) concludes that the Aramaic permits any date from the late sixth century onward and cannot decide the question, and Mitchell & Joyce (1965) that the instrument names do not exclude the sixth century.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Objections to the historical statements rest on silence and unexamined assumptions; a date "say 535 B.C." is weighed against 164 B.C. (pp. iii–vi; p. 179) [3]
- *Supporting (indirect):* Alexander was shown the Book of Daniel at Jerusalem (a tradition known only from Josephus). (Ant. 11.8.5) [9]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Persian words presuppose an established Persian empire; Greek words (kitharis, psalterion, symphonia) "demand" a date after Alexander; placement among the Writings; Ben Sira's silence; historical difficulties. (Introduction pp. xlvii–lxiii) [1]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Chs. 1–6 pre-Maccabean (3rd century); chs. 7–12 from 168–165 BC. (§21 d) [2]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Porphyry argued the book was composed in Judaea under Antiochus Epiphanes. (Prologue) [4]
- *Context (direct):* Mattathias's speech cites the three in the furnace and Daniel among the lions — the stories were known when 1 Maccabees was written (c. 100 BC). (1 Macc 2:59–60) [10]
- *Context (direct):* A copy of chs. 8–11 dated 230–160 BCE; the range reaches both before and into the Maccabean period. (4Q114 entry) [5]
- *Context (direct):* Ben Sira's list names Jeremiah, Ezekiel, the Twelve, Zerubbabel, Jeshua and Nehemiah but not Daniel — nor Ezra, which limits the weight of the silence. (Sir 49:6–13) [11]
- *Context (indirect):* The Aramaic allows any date from the late sixth to the second century; a second-century date cannot rest on three Greek words; the date "cannot be decided upon linguistic grounds alone". (pp. 31–79 (summary, p. 79)) [7]
- *Context (indirect):* The instrument names are not decisive; a sixth-century date for the orchestra "cannot be categorically denied". (pp. 19–27) [8]

*Limitations:* Only one radiocarbon date read. Pusey, Hengstenberg, Collins and Goldingay not read directly.
