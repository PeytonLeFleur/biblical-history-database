## Biblical account

Daniel 1:1–2 states that in the third year of Jehoiakim, Nebuchadnezzar king of Babylon came to Jerusalem and besieged it; the Lord gave Jehoiakim into his hand with some of the vessels of the house of God, which Nebuchadnezzar brought to the land of Shinar, to the treasure house of his god.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Babylonian expansion into the Levant after Carchemish (605–604 BC)

Jehoiakim had been installed by Necho II in 609 and ruled as an Egyptian vassal. After Carchemish (605) the Babylonian Chronicle has Nebuchadnezzar campaigning in "Hatti" (Syria-Palestine) through his accession year and receiving the tribute of "all the kings of Hatti" in his first year (604), when he also destroyed Ashkelon. The chronicle does not name Judah or Jerusalem before the capture of "the city of Judah" in 597. 2 Kings 24:1 says Jehoiakim became Nebuchadnezzar's servant for three years and then rebelled. ## Competing reconstructions

- **A 605/604 BC campaign: accession-year reckoning harmonisation** (Traditional and evangelical interpreters, e.g. D. J. Wiseman (1965); D. Guzik): Daniel counts Jehoiakim's years without the accession year (Babylonian practice) while Jeremiah counts it, so Daniel's "third year" and Jeremiah's "fourth" denote the same year, 605/604. Berossus's report of Jewish captives sent to Babylonia in the campaign of Nabopolassar's death is cited as support. (Wiseman's essay itself was not accessed.) [6, 7, 8]
- **No siege of Jerusalem before 597: Dan 1:1 reflects later tradition** (C. L. Seow (2003), J. J. Collins (1984)): Babylonian chronicles record campaigns in the Levant but no attack on Jerusalem before 597, and the biblical sources describe only the 597 siege; the author of Daniel apparently drew on 2 Chronicles 36:5–7 and 2 Kings 24:1. The chapter is part of court tales collected in the Hellenistic period. [9, 10, 11]
- **Submission without a recorded siege** (Reconstruction compatible with ABC 5 and 2 Kgs 24:1): Judah may have submitted among "all the kings of Hatti" who paid tribute in 604, without a siege recorded in the chronicle. Wikipedia, citing Eph'al (2003) and Elayi (2018), notes that no westernmost Levantine state is recorded as swearing fealty in 605 itself. [1, 2]

## Claims and evidence

### Nebuchadnezzar campaigned in Syria-Palestine ("Hatti") in 605–604 BC and received tribute from its kings.

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

ABC 5 directly records campaigning in Hatti through the accession year and the tribute of "all the kings of Hatti" in year 1 (604), with the sack of Ashkelon.

- *Supporting (direct):* In his accession year Nebuchadnezzar marched about victoriously in Hatti until Šabatu and brought its heavy tribute to Babylon; in year 1 all the kings of Hatti came before him and paid heavy tribute, and he sacked Ashkelon. (Obverse 12–20) [1]

*Limitations:* The chronicle names no Judean king for 605/604.

### Nebuchadnezzar besieged Jerusalem, or forced Jehoiakim's submission, in 605/604 BC (Dan 1:1).

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

No Babylonian source names Jerusalem before 597, and critical scholars (Seow; Collins) regard Dan 1:1 as unhistorical or derived from 2 Kings/Chronicles. Berossus reports Jewish captives in the 605 campaign, and Judah could be among the tributary "kings of Hatti" of 604; 2 Kings 24:1 has Jehoiakim become Nebuchadnezzar's servant. Whether this amounted to a siege, as Daniel says, is materially disputed.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Berossus relates that Nebuchadnezzar, on learning of his father's death, entrusted the captives taken from the Jews, Phoenicians and Syrians to friends to bring to Babylonia, where they were settled in colonies. (Against Apion 1.19) [8]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Seow (2003) notes that the Babylonian chronicles record Nebuchadnezzar's Palestinian campaigns as crown prince but none against Jerusalem, and that biblical and Babylonian sources mention an attack on Jerusalem only in 597. (Interpretation section) [9]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Citing Eph'al (2003) and Elayi (2018), the article states that after Carchemish none of the westernmost Levantine states swore fealty or paid tribute in 605; it mentions no 605 attack on Jerusalem. (Military campaigns section) [2]
- *Context (direct):* In his accession year Nebuchadnezzar marched about victoriously in Hatti until Šabatu and brought its heavy tribute to Babylon; in year 1 all the kings of Hatti came before him and paid heavy tribute, and he sacked Ashkelon. (Obverse 12–20) [1]
- *Context (contextual):* Places the Egyptian Harran campaign in Du'ûzu–Ulûlu 609, constraining Jehoiakim's accession to the second half of 609. (Year 17) [12]

*Limitations:* Wiseman and Collins were not read directly.

### Judean captives were sent to Babylonia during Nebuchadnezzar's 605 BC campaign.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Berossus (via Josephus) explicitly mentions captives "from the Jews" sent to Babylonia in the campaign during which Nabopolassar died. The report is late, transmitted second-hand, and not confirmed in cuneiform sources.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Berossus relates that Nebuchadnezzar, on learning of his father's death, entrusted the captives taken from the Jews, Phoenicians and Syrians to friends to bring to Babylonia, where they were settled in colonies. (Against Apion 1.19) [8]
- *Context (direct):* In his accession year Nebuchadnezzar marched about victoriously in Hatti until Šabatu and brought its heavy tribute to Babylon; in year 1 all the kings of Hatti came before him and paid heavy tribute, and he sacked Ashkelon. (Obverse 12–20) [1]

*Limitations:* Single late line of evidence; Berossus gives Nabopolassar 29 years against the chronicle's 21, showing transmission problems.

### Temple vessels taken from Jerusalem were deposited in the temple treasury of Nebuchadnezzar's god.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Berossus says Nebuchadnezzar adorned the temple of Belus and other temples with spoils from this war, consistent with Babylonian practice. No source mentions Jerusalem's vessels in 605; 2 Chr 36:7 and 2 Kgs 24:13 describe removals without the 605 date.

- *Context (contextual):* Berossus says Nebuchadnezzar adorned the temple of Belus and other temples out of the spoils taken in this war. (Against Apion 1.19) [8]

*Limitations:* No direct external evidence for the vessels themselves.

### Daniel's "third year of Jehoiakim" and Jeremiah's "fourth year" can denote the same period under different regnal reckonings.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

Chronology engine, 32 reckoning combinations (20 consistent with the anchors: Jehoiakim's accession 1 Aug 609 – 31 Jan 608; Carchemish Mar 605 BC – Aug 605 BC). Jeremiah 46:2 ("fourth year" = Carchemish) is consistent with: accession year · Nisan; accession year · Tishri; non-accession year · Tishri; it is inconsistent with non-accession year · Nisan. Window of Daniel's "third year" by reckoning (including uncertainty): accession year · Nisan: Feb 606 BC – May 605 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605); accession year · Tishri: Aug 607 BC – Nov 605 BC (extends past the battle into the post-Carchemish campaign); non-accession year · Tishri: Aug 608 BC – Nov 606 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605); non-accession year · Nisan: Feb 607 BC – May 606 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605). Only accession-year counting with a Tishri (autumn) new year makes the "third year" run through the summer-605 campaign after Carchemish; accession-year counting with a Nisan (Babylonian) new year ends the "third year" around Nisan 605, before the battle except at the uncertainty margin. Harmonisation (Guzik; Wiseman, not read directly) appeals to accession-year counting in Daniel; the computation shows this works naturally only with a Tishri new year. Critical scholarship (Seow; Collins) treats the discrepancy as a historical problem in Daniel. Both biblical statements are preserved unchanged.

- *Supporting (contextual):* Guzik explains the one-year difference by Babylonian accession-year counting in Daniel versus Judean counting in Jeremiah. (Commentary on 1:1) [6]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Citing Eph'al (2003) and Elayi (2018), the article states that after Carchemish none of the westernmost Levantine states swore fealty or paid tribute in 605; it mentions no 605 attack on Jerusalem. (Military campaigns section) [2]
- *Context (contextual):* Places the Egyptian Harran campaign in Du'ûzu–Ulûlu 609, constraining Jehoiakim's accession to the second half of 609. (Year 17) [12]

*Limitations:* The computation depends on the reconstructed accession window and conservative new-year windows.
