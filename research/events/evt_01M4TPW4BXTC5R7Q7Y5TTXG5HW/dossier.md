## Biblical references

- Daniel 5:28–31 (reference): Implied; Daniel does not narrate the capture.

## Biblical account

Daniel's reading of PERES announces that the kingdom is given to the Medes and Persians; the chapter ends with Belshazzar's death and Darius the Mede receiving the kingdom. The capture itself is not narrated.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Tašrītu–Arahsamna, Nabonidus year 17 (Oct 539 BC)

Cyrus defeated the army of Akkad at Opis on the Tigris; Sippar was taken on the 14th (11 Oct) without battle and Nabonidus fled; on the 16th (13 Oct in Parker & Dubberstein) Ugbaru and Cyrus's army entered Babylon without battle; Nabonidus was captured; Cyrus entered on 3 Arahsamna (29 Oct) and peace was proclaimed. [1, 2, 3]

### Rulers

- Nabonidus: Defeated king [1]
- Cyrus II (the Great): Conqueror [1]

## Competing reconstructions

- **Peaceful entry (cuneiform sources)** (Nabonidus Chronicle, Cyrus Cylinder): Babylon was entered "without battle"; the Cylinder frames this as Marduk's choice of Cyrus. [1, 3]
- **Surprise entry by the river during a festival (Greek sources)** (Herodotus 1.191, Xenophon, Cyropaedia 7.5): After a siege, Cyrus diverted the Euphrates and his men entered along the riverbed while the city celebrated; Xenophon adds that the king was killed in his palace. [5, 6]
- **Propaganda and a forced entry** (P. Briant, P. Michalowski, G. Tolini, via Wikipedia): The "without battle" claim is pro-Persian framing; Tolini proposes a forced entry through the Enlil Gate that the river story may echo. [7]

## Claims and evidence

### Cyrus's forces under Ugbaru entered Babylon on 16 Tašrītu of Nabonidus's 17th year (12/13 Oct 539 BC), and Cyrus entered on 3 Arahsamna (29 Oct).

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Dated directly by the Nabonidus Chronicle; converted by Parker & Dubberstein's tables; the Cyrus Cylinder confirms the entry. The one-day difference between 12 and 13 October reflects conversion conventions, not a source conflict.

- *Supporting (direct):* Sippar taken on the 14th; on the 16th Ugbaru and Cyrus's army entered Babylon without a battle; Nabonidus was arrested; Cyrus entered on 3 Arahsamna; Ugbaru died on 11 Arahsamna. (iii 12–22) [1]
- *Supporting (direct):* VII/14/17 = 11 Oct 539, VII/16/17 = 13 Oct, VIII/3 = 29 Oct; a Uruk tablet dated by Nabonidus on the following day shows news had not yet arrived. (p. 11) [2]
- *Supporting (direct):* Marduk made Cyrus enter Babylon "without fighting or battle". (Line 17) [3]

*Limitations:* The year is broken on the tablet and restored from context.

### Babylon was entered without a battle.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: evidence

Both cuneiform sources say "without battle", but both are Persian-period and the Cylinder is royal propaganda. Herodotus describes a siege and surprise entry; Xenophon a fight at the palace. Briant and Michalowski doubt the cuneiform claim (via Wikipedia).

- *Supporting (direct):* Sippar taken on the 14th; on the 16th Ugbaru and Cyrus's army entered Babylon without a battle; Nabonidus was arrested; Cyrus entered on 3 Arahsamna; Ugbaru died on 11 Arahsamna. (iii 12–22) [1]
- *Supporting (direct):* Marduk made Cyrus enter Babylon "without fighting or battle". (Line 17) [3]
- *Challenging (indirect):* The Babylonians fought, were shut up in a siege, and the city fell by surprise when Cyrus diverted the river. (1.190–191) [5]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Cyrus's men killed guards and the king in the palace, and cavalry cleared the streets. (7.5.26–33) [6]
- *Context (indirect):* Briant calls a battle-free entry "prima facie unlikely"; Tolini proposes a forced entry through the Enlil Gate. (Invasion; historiography) [7]

*Limitations:* Modern discussions (Briant, Tolini) not read directly.

### Nabonidus was captured after the fall of Babylon.

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

The Chronicle records that Nabonidus fled and was later arrested in Babylon.

- *Supporting (direct):* Sippar taken on the 14th; on the 16th Ugbaru and Cyrus's army entered Babylon without a battle; Nabonidus was arrested; Cyrus entered on 3 Arahsamna; Ugbaru died on 11 Arahsamna. (iii 12–22) [1]
