# Battle of Carchemish (605 BC)

## Biblical references

- Jeremiah 46:2 (reference): Superscription of the oracle against Egypt.

## Biblical account

Jeremiah 46:2 heads an oracle about Egypt by identifying the army of Pharaoh Necho that Nebuchadnezzar defeated at Carchemish on the Euphrates, dating the defeat to the fourth year of Jehoiakim son of Josiah.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Late Iron Age; end of the Assyrian-Egyptian alliance (609–605 BC)

After the fall of Nineveh and Harran, Egypt under Necho II held the upper Euphrates. In Nabopolassar's 21st year (605) the crown prince Nebuchadnezzar crossed the river, defeated the Egyptian army at Carchemish, pursued its remnants into the district of Hamath, and then returned to Babylon on his father's death to take the throne on 1 Ululu (7 Sep 605). [1, 2, 3]

### Rulers

- Nebuchadnezzar II: Babylonian commander (crown prince), king from Sep 605 [1]
- Necho II: Egyptian king whose army was defeated [1]
- Nabopolassar: Reigning king of Babylon (died 8 Abu 605) [1]

## Claims and evidence

### Nebuchadnezzar defeated the Egyptian army at Carchemish in 605 BC.

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

The Babylonian Chronicle (ABC 5), a near-contemporary cuneiform record, directly reports the battle in Nabopolassar's 21st year. Berossus independently recalls the campaign. Jeremiah 46:2 names the same battle.

- *Supporting (direct):* The chronicle records that in Nabopolassar's 21st year the crown prince Nebuchadnezzar crossed the Euphrates, defeated the Egyptian army at Carchemish and pursued its survivors into the district of Hamath. (Obverse 1–8) [1]
- *Supporting (indirect):* Berossus (via Josephus) describes Nebuchadnezzar, sent by his father against a rebel governor in the west, defeating him and reducing the country, when news of Nabopolassar's death recalled him. (Against Apion 1.19) [5]

*Limitations:* Translation read via Livius, not the printed edition.

### Jeremiah 46:2's dating of the battle to Jehoiakim's fourth year is consistent with the Babylonian date.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Chronology engine, 32 reckoning combinations (20 consistent with the anchors: Jehoiakim's accession 1 Aug 609 – 31 Jan 608; Carchemish Mar 605 BC – Aug 605 BC). Jeremiah 46:2 ("fourth year" = Carchemish) is consistent with: accession year · Nisan; accession year · Tishri; non-accession year · Tishri; it is inconsistent with non-accession year · Nisan. Window of Daniel's "third year" by reckoning (including uncertainty): accession year · Nisan: Feb 606 BC – May 605 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605); accession year · Tishri: Aug 607 BC – Nov 605 BC (extends past the battle into the post-Carchemish campaign); non-accession year · Tishri: Aug 608 BC – Nov 606 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605); non-accession year · Nisan: Feb 607 BC – May 606 BC (closes before the latest possible battle date, Aug 605). Only accession-year counting with a Tishri (autumn) new year makes the "third year" run through the summer-605 campaign after Carchemish; accession-year counting with a Nisan (Babylonian) new year ends the "third year" around Nisan 605, before the battle except at the uncertainty margin.

- *Context (contextual):* The Egyptian army's Harran campaign of Du'ûzu–Ulûlu 609 bounds the date of Josiah's death and hence Jehoiakim's accession. (Year 17) [2]

*Limitations:* Nisan/Tishri windows are conservative approximations (Parker & Dubberstein not imported). Jehoiakim's accession window is a reconstruction.
