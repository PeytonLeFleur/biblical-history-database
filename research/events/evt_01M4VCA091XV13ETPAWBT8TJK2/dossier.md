## Biblical account

In Josiah's days Pharaoh Necoh went up "to"/"against" the king of Assyria to the river Euphrates; Josiah went to meet him and Necoh killed him at Megiddo when he saw him; his servants carried him dead in a chariot to Jerusalem and buried him; the people of the land anointed Jehoahaz.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Spring–summer 609 BC

ABC 3: in Nabopolassar's 16th year (610/609) the Babylonians and Medes took Harran from Aššur-uballiṭ and "the army of Egypt that had come to help him"; in the 17th year, in Du'ûzu (June/July 609), Aššur-uballiṭ "with a large army from Egypt" crossed the Euphrates and besieged Harran until Ulûlu without success. Necho's march north is this campaign. Josephus says Necho was marching "to fight with the Medes and Babylonians". [1, 2]

## Competing reconstructions

- **Necho marching to help Assyria against Babylon** (ABC 3 (Egyptian army with Aššur-uballiṭ, 610–609), Josephus (against the Medes and Babylonians), D. Guzik (NKJV)): Josiah tried to stop an Egyptian army going to Assyria's aid. [1, 2, 6]
- **Necho marching against Assyria (610)** (J. R. Lumby (1887), before ABC 3 was published): Read the Hebrew as "against" and supposed Josiah an ally or tributary of Assyria. Superseded by the chronicle on the direction of the campaign. [7]
- **Herodotus's battle at "Magdolos" against "the Syrians" is this battle** (C. F. Keil (1872): a confusion with Megiddo, F. C. Movers (as reported by Keil): Migdol on the Egyptian frontier): Herodotus 2.159 has Necho defeat the Syrians at Magdolos and then take Cadytis. [8, 9]

## Claims and evidence

### In 610–609 BC Egypt sent armies to support Assyria against Babylon, and in summer 609 an Egyptian army with Aššur-uballiṭ crossed the Euphrates against Harran.

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

ABC 3 records the Egyptian army helping Aššur-uballiṭ in year 16 and crossing the Euphrates with him in Du'ûzu of year 17; Josephus, independently of the chronicle, says Necho marched to fight the Medes and Babylonians. Lumby's pre-1923 reading ("against the king of Assyria") is superseded on this point.

- *Supporting (direct):* Aššur-uballiṭ and "the army of Egypt that had come to help him" abandoned Harran; in Du'ûzu "with a large army from Egypt" crossed the Euphrates and besieged Harran until Ulûlu. (years 16–17) [1]
- *Supporting (direct):* Neco marched to the Euphrates "to fight with the Medes and Babylonians". (Ant. 10.5.1) [2]
- *Challenging (indirect):* The expedition was "against the king of Assyria" in 610 (written before the chronicle was published). (on 23:29) [7]

### Josiah died in 609 BC, during Necho's march north and before the Egyptian crossing of the Euphrates in Du'ûzu.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Kings places the death during Necho's march to the Euphrates; ABC 3 dates that campaign's crossing to Du'ûzu 609; the three months of Jehoahaz and the installation of Jehoiakim follow (2 Kgs 23:31–34), consistent with Jehoiakim's fourth year = 605 (Jer 46:2; ABC 5). The chronicle does not name Josiah. Classified historically plausible rather than strongly supported because only one independent line (ABC 3) supports the year directly.

- *Supporting (direct):* The 609 crossing in Du'ûzu. (year 17) [1]
- *Context (direct):* Josiah reigned 31 years; Jehoahaz three months and ten days, deposed as Necho returned. (Ant. 10.5.1–2) [2]

*Limitations:* Indirect synchronism: the chronicle does not mention Judah.

### Josiah died at Megiddo (2 Kgs 23:29) rather than after being brought back to Jerusalem.

**Classification:** Disputed (requires review) · layer: reconstruction

Kings: Necho "killed him at Megiddo" and his servants "carried him dead". 2 Chr 35:23–24: wounded by archers, moved to a second chariot, brought to Jerusalem, "and he died". Josephus: he retreated to Jerusalem and "died of that wound". Keil holds that Chronicles does not say where he died; Lumby reports a view, from Zech 12:11, that he died at Hadad-rimmon. No external source bears on it.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Chronicles does not affirm that he died in Jerusalem. (p. 496) [8]
- *Challenging (direct):* Sorely wounded, he returned to Jerusalem and died of the wound. (Ant. 10.5.1) [2]
- *Context (indirect):* Some suppose he died at Hadad-rimmon (Zech 12:11). (on 23:30) [7]

### Herodotus's battle at "Magdolos" against "the Syrians" (2.159) is Necho's battle at Megiddo.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: reconstruction

Herodotus places a land victory of Necho at Magdolos followed by the capture of Cadytis. Keil explains Magdolos as a confusion with Megiddo; Movers (via Keil) placed it at Migdol on the Egyptian frontier. Herodotus is a fifth-century Greek source dependent on Egyptian informants.

- *Supporting (direct):* Necos engaged battle at Magdolos with the Syrians and conquered them; then took Cadytis. (2.159) [9]
- *Supporting (indirect):* Magdolos arises from confusion with Megiddo. (p. 495) [8]
- *Challenging (indirect):* Movers transferred the battle to Magdolo on the Egyptian frontier. (pp. 494–495 n.) [8]

### Megiddo is Tel Megiddo (Tell al-Mutesellim).

**Classification:** Independently corroborated (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Pleiades associates the names Megiddo and Magidû with Tel Megiddo with certainty; Keil gives the same identification via Legio/Lejjun.

- *Supporting (direct):* Tel Megiddo, Magidû, Megiddo (certain). (names) [4]
- *Supporting (indirect):* Megiddo is identical with Roman Legio (Lejjun). (p. 495) [8]
