# The accusers punished; Darius's decree honouring the God of Daniel (Dan 6:24–27)

> Programmatic validation is not historical verification. Classifications are machine-proposed unless marked as owner-reviewed.

## Biblical references

- Daniel 6:24–27 (narration)

## Biblical account

The king has Daniel's accusers thrown into the den with their children and wives. He then writes to all peoples that in all his dominion men should tremble before the God of Daniel, the living God whose kingdom will not be destroyed.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Reign of Darius the Mede (undated)

Collective punishment of families is attributed to Persian kings by Greek and Roman writers: Herodotus has Darius I arrest Intaphrenes with his sons and kinsmen, and Ammianus says Persian law put to death all the relatives of a guilty man. Deuteronomy 24:16 forbids this in Israelite law. [1, 2]

## Claims and evidence

### Persian kings punished the families of the condemned along with them.

**Classification:** Historically plausible (machine proposed) · layer: evidence

Herodotus reports Darius I seizing Intaphrenes with his sons and kinsmen; Ammianus (4th century AD) describes Persian laws putting all relatives of a guilty man to death. Both are outside observers, and neither concerns Darius the Mede.

- *Supporting (indirect):* Darius I put Intaphrenes "and his sons and all his kinsmen" in bonds for execution. (3.119) [1]
- *Supporting (indirect):* Persian laws provide that "because of the guilt of a single person all his relatives are put to death". (23.6.81) [2]

*Limitations:* Greek and Roman sources, not Persian legal texts.

### Darius issued an empire-wide decree honouring the God of Daniel.

**Classification:** No independent corroboration found (machine proposed) · layer: text

No such decree is known outside Daniel.
