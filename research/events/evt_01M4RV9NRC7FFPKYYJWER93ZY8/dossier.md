## Biblical references

- Daniel 4:4–27 (narration)

## Biblical account

Nebuchadnezzar, writing in the first person, recounts a dream of a vast tree cut down at the command of a holy watcher, its stump bound in the field while "seven times" pass. His diviners fail; Daniel interprets the tree as the king, who will be driven out to live with the animals until he acknowledges that the Most High rules, and urges him to break off his sins by righteousness.

## Historical setting

**Period:** Reign of Nebuchadnezzar II (undated in the text)

The tree image draws on biblical imagery of rulers as great trees (Ezekiel 31). The dream is undated; the chapter is framed as a royal proclamation. [1]
